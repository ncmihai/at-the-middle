If midspot had a mascot city, it would be Bologna. When we started ranking destinations by "everyone arrives within a day and nobody goes broke", Bologna kept appearing at the top of the list for group after group. Cheap flights from every corner of Europe, a location in the middle of Italy's north, and a city that is genuinely great to be in — not just a compromise everyone tolerates. We have been there enough times now to know it deserves its own post.

## Why groups keep landing there

Geography does half the work. Bologna sits on the main Rome–Milan rail and motorway axis, with fast trains to Florence (35 minutes), Venice (about 1.5 hours), Milan (1 hour) and Rome (2 hours). Guglielmo Marconi airport (BLQ) is served by low-cost carriers from dozens of European cities, and the city is 40 minutes by train from Florence's airport if someone's flight lands there instead. In practice: a group flying from, say, Barcelona, Berlin, London and Bucharest can all be in Bologna on the same Friday afternoon, with the biggest arrival gap measured in hours, not days. That is exactly the scenario [midspot](/) was built for — enter everyone's origins and dates, and see which city lands the group together.

The second reason is that Bologna is not a sacrifice for anyone. It is the kind of city where the person who wanted Rome and the person who wanted Venice both end up happy, because the food is the main event anyway.

## Forty kilometres of porticoes

Bologna has around 40 km of porticoes — covered walkways along the streets — which sounds like a detail until you are walking them in the rain at 9 a.m. with a coffee in hand and realizing you never have to open an umbrella. The most famous stretch leads up to the Sanctuary of the Madonna di San Luca: 666 arches (give or take, depending on how you count) climbing the hill above the city, about 3.8 km and 45 minutes on foot. Do it once. Take the bus back down. The porticoes also mean the city center is entirely walkable — Piazza Maggiore, the Two Towers, the Quadrilatero market streets — all under cover.

## The food: the real versions

Let's be direct: Bologna is where you eat the real tagliatelle al ragù, the real tortellini in brodo, and the real mortadella — the ones other cities serve tourists are inspired by these. The tagliatelle here is hand-cut, wide and rough, made to hold the ragù; the tortellini are small and folded around a pork-longanica filling and served in capon broth; the mortadella is sliced thick and eaten as it is, from the counters of the Quadrilatero. Add tigelle with lard, crescentine, tagliatelle... and the aperitivo culture, which in Bologna is a serious affair — order a spritz between 18:00 and 20:00 and the bar lays out a buffet.

Our honest advice: book one proper trattoria dinner (the queue outside is usually a good sign), do one market lunch in the Quadrilatero, and leave the rest to wandering. The university crowd keeps prices honest and the aperitivo generous.

## The Two Towers and the university vibe

The Asinelli and Garina towers — you can climb the Asinelli's 498 steps for the best view over the red roofs — lean over Piazza di Porta Ravegnana like a medieval postcard. And because one of Europe's oldest universities is here, the city has a young, loud, always-somewhere-to-be energy: the Piazza Verdi area in the evenings, the student bars near Via Zamboni, the general feeling that dinner starts at 21:00. It makes Bologna feel alive at night without being exhausting.

## The practical part: budget and group logistics

Here is a realistic per-person budget for a long weekend in Bologna, in EUR:

- **Flights**: €40–140 return from most of Europe with low-cost carriers if booked 3–6 weeks ahead; Ryanair, Wizz Air and the legacy carriers all serve BLQ. Book early — prices jump fast for Friday arrivals.
- **Getting in**: the Marconi Express monorail to the station is about €12.50, or the regular bus for €2; many hotels are a 10-minute walk from the station.
- **Stay**: €50–90 per night for a nice apartment or B&B in the center (the area around Via dell'Indipendenza or Santo Stefano is ideal). Three nights: €150–270.
- **Food and drink**: €30–50 per day if you eat well — Bologna is pricier than the south but still reasonable; a pasta at a trattoria is €10–14, aperitivo with buffet €10–12, coffee at the bar €1.50.
- **Activities and trains**: most of the city is free to wander; towers and museums €5–12; a day trip to Florence or Modena by train €15–25 return.
- **Total per person**: **€300–550** for a long weekend all in — and if your group arrives from different airports on the same day, the shared taxi or the train from Florence/Milan evens out quickly.

Best time: spring (April–June) and autumn (September–October); August is hot and many locals leave, which actually makes it cheaper and quieter. December's Christmas markets under the porticoes are worth the cold.
