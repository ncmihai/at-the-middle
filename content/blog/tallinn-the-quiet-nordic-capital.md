Some cities shout. Tallinn whispers — and somehow that makes it one of the most pleasant capitals in Europe. We went expecting a pretty old town and a couple of viewpoints, and left wondering why everyone we know was still booking Riga or Vilnius instead. If you have never considered Tallinn as a meeting point for your group, this is the post that changes that.

## A UNESCO old town on a hill, entirely walkable

The Old Town of Tallinn is one of the best-preserved medieval walled towns in Europe, and it sits on a hill above the port, which means two things: the views from the upper town viewpoints (Patarei, Kohtuotsa, Rahukoht) over the red roofs and the sea are genuinely stunning, and the whole thing is compact enough to cross on foot in twenty minutes. There is no need for transport inside the walls — the Lower Town's lanes, the Town Hall Square with its pharmacy museum, the St. Olaf's church tower climb: all of it is a pleasant wander, not a hike.

What makes it feel different from other Baltic capitals is the mix. You have the Hanseatic merchant houses and the grey Nordic baroque sitting next to each other, and beyond the walls the city opens into Kalamaja — wooden houses, cafés, and the Telliskivi Creative City, a former factory complex that is now the creative heart of the city: design shops, street food, concerts, and a market hall that is perfect for a rainy afternoon. The baroque-neoclassical Kadriorg palace and park on the eastern edge is where locals actually go on weekends.

## A digital-first country (yes, really)

Estonia runs on the internet. Free Wi-Fi is everywhere, including most beaches; you can vote, file taxes, start a company and get married online; and since 2014 the country has offered e-residency to non-residents, which tells you everything about the mindset. Practically, for a visitor this means: mobile tickets, contactless everything, apps for parking and public transport, and a city where the tourist office can solve most things by email in minutes. It feels like a small preview of how a country would work if it were designed today.

And because it is Nordic-adjacent, it is cheap for Nordic visitors specifically: Finns take the ferry from Helsinki (about two hours, often €20–40 return if booked early) and spend the weekend like it is an extension of their own city — which, in price terms, almost is.

## Saunas, sea and other Tallinn habits

Two things we did not expect and now recommend to everyone. First, the sea: the city beach at Pirita is a ten-minute bus ride from the Old Town, with pine forest behind it and, in summer, water warm enough for a proper swim. Second, the sauna culture — yes, you can book a public sauna right by the water (the most famous is at the end of the Katarina pier, with a view back over the old town roofs) and do it properly: hot room, a dip in the Baltic if you are brave, repeat. In winter that dip is a whole different conversation — we watched locals do it, and we are still not sure we will join them. Even if you skip the swim, an evening sauna with a view of the ferries is one of the cheapest luxuries in Northern Europe.

## The practical part: budget, getting there, and a 2–3 day sketch

Here is what we actually budget per person for a 2–3 day trip to Tallinn, in EUR:

- **Getting there**: direct flights from most of Northern and Central Europe run €40–120 return with low-cost carriers if booked a few weeks ahead; from Western Europe count €90–180. The Helsinki–Tallinn ferry is €20–40 each way and a lovely way to arrive. From the airport, the tram or bus to the Old Town costs about €2 and takes 20–25 minutes.
- **Stay**: €40–75 per night for a good apartment or hotel inside or just outside the walls. Two to three nights: €80–225.
- **Food and drink**: €20–35 per day. Lunch in a daily-menu spot is €8–12; dinner with a drink in Kalamaja or Telliskivi €15–25. Grocery stores (Rimi, Selver) are cheap if you self-cater breakfast.
- **Activities**: mostly free or cheap — the old town and viewpoints are free, churches and towers €5–10, a sauna by the sea (yes, really) €15–30.
- **Total per person**: **€250–450** for 2–3 days, depending mostly on flights.

A realistic sketch: day one, arrive, drop bags, walk the Lower Town and Town Hall Square, dinner in a beer cellar. Day two, climb the upper town viewpoints in the morning, Kalamaja and Telliskivi in the afternoon, sunset from Rahukoht. Day three (if you have it), Kadriorg palace and park, a sauna by the sea, or a long café morning and a last walk on the walls. That is the Tallinn pace — we do not recommend rushing it.

A few practical notes we wish someone had told us: the Tallinn Card (city pass) only pays off if you visit several paid museums in one day — for most visitors the free old town plus one or two paid towers is the sweet spot. Tipping is appreciated but not mandatory (round up or leave 5–10%). And if you are there in late November or December, the Christmas market in the Town Hall Square is small, pretty and completely walkable — mulled wine included, obviously.
